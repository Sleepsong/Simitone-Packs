# Open for Business

Businesses for The Sims 1, after **The Sims 2: Open for Business**. Nothing from The Sims 2 is used:
this is its design, built again on The Sims 1's engine, following its rules where they are known.
How it maps onto Open for Business is in [What came from Open for Business](#what-came-from-open-for-business).

**None of it has been played yet**: see [State](#state).

## Selling

- **Put an object up for sale.** In your family's home (or a business lot, below), click an object
  from the buy catalog and choose **Business > Sell**, then a price:

  | Price | Of the catalog price |
  |---|---|
  | Ridiculously Cheap | 50% |
  | Cheap | 75% |
  | Average | 100% |
  | Expensive | 125% |
  | Very Expensive | 150% |
  | Custom Price... | type it |

  A priced object's menu has **For Sale (§price)** with **Change Price** and **Take Off Sale**.
  Pointing at it shows its price and the catalog's. Objects for sale stay usable.
- **Visitors browse.** Neighbours who are visiting look at what is for sale now and then. One who
  decides to buy walks over, pays, and the object leaves the lot with the buy-mode ca-ching.
  Each time a visitor looks:

  | Price against the catalog's | Chance they buy |
  |---|---|
  | Half or less | 95% |
  | Three quarters | about 73% |
  | The same | 50% |
  | One and a half times | 25% |
  | Twice or more | never |

  Their loyalty, a tended till and perks add to that (below). A visitor who does not buy keeps the
  object in mind, which is what a sales pitch works on, and looks again about a minute later.
- **Open and closed.** Click your own Sim (or the till) and choose **Business > Close for Business**
  or **Open for Business**. Closed, nobody new shops and employees are not paid; customers already
  buying are still served. A business is open until you close it.

Only objects in the buy catalog can be sold, and only visiting neighbours buy: not the family, pets,
or the maid, the burglar and other NPCs. Something in use, or with something on it, is not taken
until it is free.

## Sales pitches

As in Open for Business, a customer can be talked into buying. Click a customer and choose **Sales**:

| Pitch | Needs | Buy meter if it works | If it fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sell | nothing | +25 | -5 |
| Offer Discount | Bronze in Sales | +35, and they pay 10% less | -5 |
| Hard Sell | Silver in Sales | +45 | -20, and a star |
| Dazzle | Gold in Sales | +70 | -35, and a star |
| Manipulate | the Manipulation perk | fills it | always works, and costs two stars |

Your Sim walks over and makes the pitch. A full meter (100) means the customer buys what they had in
mind. Whether a pitch works depends on the pitch, your Sim's Sales badge, the customer's stars and
mood, and the price: a fair price is easier to sell. You hear whether it worked, and pointing at the
customer shows their buy meter.

## Talent badges

Three of Open for Business's badges, each **Bronze, Silver or Gold**, earned by doing the work:

| Badge | Practice | What it does (none / Bronze / Silver / Gold) |
|---|---|---|
| Cashiering | each customer rung up at the till | ringing up takes about 4 / 3 / 2 / 1 seconds |
| Restocking | each object restocked | putting one back takes about 6 / 4 / 2 / 1 seconds |
| Sales | each sales pitch | unlocks Offer Discount / Hard Sell / Dazzle, and each level makes a pitch 5 points likelier to work |

Bronze comes at 8 points, Silver at 25 and Gold at 60. A badge belongs to the Sim, family or
neighbour, and goes with them from lot to lot. You are told when one is earned. `business badges`
shows the badges of the family and staff on the lot, with the points to go.

The other Open for Business badges (Toy Making, Robotics, Flower Arranging, Cosmetology) need its
crafting stations and makeover chair, which a pack of only code cannot add.

## The till

Choose **Business > Use as Till** on any catalog object: a counter, a desk, a cash register if you
have one. Its menu has **Tend**, **Restock**, **Open / Close for Business** and **Stop Using as Till**.

With a till, a customer who decides to buy goes to it and waits to be rung up by one of the family
or a cashier standing within two tiles of it, one customer at a time, the longest wait first. The
best Cashiering badge there sets the speed. A customer nobody serves within about two minutes gives
up and thinks less of the business. While someone is at the till, visitors look at things twice as
often and are 15 points more likely to buy. Without a till, customers go to the object and leave the
money.

## Restocking

The business remembers each object that sells: what it was, where it stood and its price. Choose
**Restock** on the till or your own Sim, and your Sim goes from empty spot to empty spot, buying each
object again at the catalog's price (less any Wholesale perk) and putting it back for sale at the price
it sold for, as fast as their Restocking badge allows. So selling above the catalog's price is profit.
A spot stays empty if the family cannot afford it or something is now in the way.

`business restock` puts everything back at once, with nobody doing the work and nobody's badge
improving. `business forget` forgets the empty spots, for things you do not want back.

## Employees

Click a visiting neighbour and choose **Business > Hire**, as a **Cashier**, **Restocker** or
**Salesperson**. A business can have eight, as in Open for Business.

- **Cashiers** go to the till and ring customers up.
- **Restockers** restock whenever something has sold out and the family can afford it.
- **Salespeople** go to customers and pitch, the biggest pitch their badge allows and the
  customer's loyalty will bear.

Employees learn badges at their work. **Pay**: each hour of the game's clock, while the business is
open, each employee on the lot is paid their wage. What they ask starts at §15 an hour and rises
§10 with every badge level they hold, so pay them more as they learn: one paid less than they ask
for 4 hours of work in a row quits, as in Open for Business, and one the family cannot pay leaves.
An employee's menu has **Job**, **Pay** (Pay What They Ask, Raise, Cut) and **Dismiss**, and pointing
at them shows their job, wage and what they ask. `business staff` lists them all.

An employee works whenever they are on the lot. This pack cannot call a Sim to the lot.

## Customers, rank and perks

Each customer has between -5 and 5 stars for the business, as in Open for Business:

| What happens | Stars |
|---|---|
| They buy at three quarters of the catalog's price or less | +2 |
| They buy at up to the catalog's price | +1 |
| They look at something at one and a half times the catalog's price or more, and do not buy | -1 |
| A Hard Sell or Dazzle fails on them | -1 |
| They give up waiting at the till | -2 |
| They are Manipulated | -2 |

Each star makes that customer 4 points more likely to buy (and a star below none, less). All the
stars together are the business's standing, which gives its **rank**, with Open for Business's names
and thresholds:

| Rank | Name | Stars |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | Visionary Vending Venture | 125 |
| 9 | Outstanding Outlet | 100 |
| 8 | Esteemed Establishment | 75 |
| 7 | Unique Boutique | 60 |
| 6 | Empowered Emporium | 45 |
| 5 | Proficient Purveyor | 30 |
| 4 | Burgeoning Bazaar | 20 |
| 3 | Fledgling Firm | 12 |
| 2 | Budding Brokerage | 7 |
| 1 | Tenderfoot Trader | 3 |
| 0 | Middling Merchant | 0 |
| -1 to -4 | | -3, -10, -20, -35 |
| -5 | Bankrupt Bargain Basement | -50 |

The rank falls when customers think less of the business. **Each new rank brings a perk point**, up
to 10, as in Open for Business. A point not spent is out of reach while the rank is below it, so
spend them as they come; perks already taken are kept. `business perks` shows the five trees and
`business perk <tree>` takes the next perk in one:

| Tree | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cash** (`cash`), paid once | LeTourneau Prize §1,000 | Valued Client Rebate §2,500 | Chamber of Commerce Prize §10,000 | Owners Association Award §20,000 | Will Wright Grant §50,000 |
| **Wholesale** (`wholesale`), off restocking | Wholesale Discount 12% | Supplier Partnership 15% | Bargain Hunter 19% | Serious Negotiator 24% | Shark of Sharks 30% |
| **Perception** (`perception`) | Assess Mood: see a customer's mood | Assess Desire: see what they want; the first pitch on it fills more | Look for Mark: your Sim finds the likeliest buyer and pitches | Convincing Personality: pitches 10 points likelier | Manipulation: the Manipulate pitch |
| **Motivation** (`motivation`) | Simply Influential\*: employees ask 10% less | Perk Up: +30 Fun and Social to a Sim, once an hour each | Motivational Speech: a Sim's badges a level higher for 3 hours | Boundless Influence\*: employees bear low pay twice as long | Rally Forth!: the family and staff +40 Energy, Comfort, Fun, Social, at a cost to your Sim |
| **Connections** (`connections`) | Notable Reputation\*: new customers start at 1 star | Sterling Reputation\*: new customers start at 2 stars | Network\*: visitors browse 1.5 times as often | Head for Numbers\*: regulars (3+ stars) 10 points likelier to buy | Power Network\*: visitors browse twice as often |

Perks marked \* are adapted. Open for Business's Motivation perks raise the owner's Influence and its
Connections perks start relationships higher and introduce Sims; The Sims 1 has no Influence, and
these work on employees and customers instead. Perk Up, Motivational Speech and Rally Forth! are on a
Sim's **Business** menu, and Look for Mark and Rally Forth! on your own Sim's.

Each home and each business lot has its own customers, rank, perks and staff.

## A business lot

- **Buy a lot.** Send one of the family to a community lot. `business` says what it costs and
  `business buy` buys it: the lot's value as the neighbourhood has it, at least §5,000. A lot can
  belong to one family.
- **Run it like home.** Whenever one of the family is on a lot they own, everything above works
  there, and the other Sims on the lot are the customers.
- **Stock it from the neighbourhood.** There is no buy mode on a lot you are visiting. To change what
  the shop holds, edit the lot from the neighbourhood, then visit and price the new things.
- **It stays as you left it.** What sold is still gone on your next visit, and prices, the till, the
  staff and the takings are kept.
- **Sell it.** `business sell`, on the lot, gives back what the family paid. The lot goes back to how
  it was built; its customers and rank are remembered.

Another family visiting an owned lot sees it as it was built, and cannot buy it.

## The books

`business` in the console (Ctrl+Shift+C) is the ledger: open or closed, rank and stars, perk points,
takings, staff, the till and who is at it, what is for sale, what has sold out, and the latest news.
Also `business perks`, `business perk <tree>`, `business staff`, `business badges`, `business open`,
`business close`, `business restock`, `business forget`, `business buy` and `business sell`.

Pointing at a Sim adds what the business knows: a customer's stars, buy meter, and (with the
perks) mood and what they want; an employee's job, pay and badges; the family's badges.

## State

It builds, and its patches apply to the `addon-packs` build of Simitone (all five target methods are
found and patched). Its rules are checked by a small test of 70 checks: prices, ranks, perk points,
perks, badges, pitches, wages, and the saved book, including the files of every earlier version.
**It has not been run with the game**, because that needs a copy of The Sims. The parts that only
playing can show:

- That visiting neighbours are the Sims with person type 1, as the engine's code suggests, and that
  their numbers stay the same from visit to visit (customers, employees and badges are remembered by
  them).
- That a Sim walks where they are sent. If the walk cannot be queued, a customer counts as arrived
  after about 45 seconds, a pitch is made from where the seller stands after about 20, and a restock
  likewise, so nothing depends on it.
- That a restocked object goes back exactly as it was, above all one that stood on a table or shelf.
- That the business's entries appear on Sims' menus, your own Sim's included.
- That changing a Sim's Fun, Social, Energy and Comfort (Perk Up, Motivational Speech, Rally Forth!)
  sits well with the game's own motives.
- On a business lot: that the game keeps the family's money when the Sim goes home, and that taking
  out the sold objects as the lot loads leaves it sound.

There is nothing in the world to mark an empty spot or show a badge or buy meter over a Sim's head:
that would need new objects and graphics, and this pack has only code. The menus, tooltips and the
`business` command say instead.

The terminal shows sales, pitches, badges, staff news and restocks in lines starting with `[business]`.

## How it works

The rules are plain sums with nothing of the game in them, so they can be tested without it:
`Reputation.cs` (stars, ranks, perks, buying, wages), `Badges.cs` (badges and the neighbourhood's
badge book) and `Sales.cs` (pitches). `Book.cs` is the business's saved book. `Shop.cs` is the
business running on a lot, and changes nothing in the engine.

What is for sale is a field the engine already has, `VMMultitileGroup.SalePrice`, which FreeSO added
for its player shops. It is already written into a lot's save and The Sims 1 never sets it, so prices
need no save data of their own. FreeSO also disables a priced object, which stops anyone using it and
stops its menu opening. This pack leaves that alone, so the family can use what is on show and
reprice it from its menu.

`OpenForBusinessPack.cs` is where the business meets the game, as Harmony patches:

1. A pie menu has been built: add the entries. They are not in any object's files, so they use an
   interaction number no object has, with a parameter that says which entry it is.
2. An interaction is asked for: if it is one of those, this pack carries it out.
3. The lot ticks: the business runs. Walking uses the engine's own "go here" marker.
4. The lot screen updates: show a price, the till, or what the business knows about a Sim.

The business's book is this pack's own data for each lot, kept with `LotPackData`: the totals, the
till (objects keep their numbers in a lot's save), the sold-out spots, the customers' stars, the rank
and perks, open or closed, and the staff. The badges are this pack's data for the neighbourhood, kept
the same way beside its `Neighborhood.iff`. With the pack off, prices stay in the save and do nothing.

A business lot is different, because the game never saves a lot that is being visited: it is loaded
from its file each time, as it was built. So the lot's file is left alone, and the book also holds who
owns the lot, what is priced and what has sold, each by the object's number in the file. The book and
the badges are written whenever they change, and the book is put back onto the lot each time the
owners visit: sold objects are taken out and prices put on. An object restocked during a visit lasts
for that visit; next time the one in the file is simply left in place. If the lot has been rebuilt and
an object is no longer where the book says, that entry is dropped.
